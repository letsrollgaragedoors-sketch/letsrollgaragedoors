Spring Replacement: The Most Common Repair

A broken torsion spring is the number one reason a garage door stops working. Replacement starts at $299 for a single spring and $469 for a two-spring system. We always suggest replacing both springs at once, since they wear at about the same rate. Two visits cost more than one, and by the time the first spring breaks the second is usually close behind.

Opener Repair vs. New Installation

Opener problems run from small fixes, like realigning a sensor or reprogramming a remote, at $75 to $125, up to a full motor replacement at $150 to $250. If your opener is old and the repair bill is getting close to the price of a new one, a new installation starting at $459 usually makes more sense. Newer openers are quieter, smarter and more reliable than ones from 10 or more years ago.

Cable Replacement

Lift cables run from the bottom corners of the door up to the spring drums. They come in pairs, and we replace both at the same time. Cable replacement runs $100 to $200 and includes re-tensioning the spring system afterward. A snapped cable often goes hand in hand with a spring problem, so we check the springs on any cable call.

Roller Replacement

Worn plastic rollers get overlooked all the time. For $99 and up for a full set, roller replacement is one of the best deals on this list. Most people notice how much quieter the door is the moment we finish. We use nylon rollers as our standard upgrade over the plastic ones most doors come with from the factory.

Emergency Service Fees

Most companies, including us, add $50 to $100 to the repair for after-hours or weekend calls. We tell you about it before we dispatch anyone, so you know the full expected cost before the technician arrives.

When to Repair vs. Replace

If your door is 15 to 20 years old and the repairs would cost more than half the price of a new door, replacing it is usually the smarter move in the long run. Our technicians will give you an honest comparison before you decide. We would rather lose a repair job and keep your trust than sell you a repair on a door that really needs to be replaced.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover garage door repair?
It depends on the cause. Damage from a storm, fire or a car hitting the door is often covered, while normal wear and tear from age usually isn't. Check your own policy before you assume anything.
How much does emergency garage door repair cost after hours?
Most companies, including us, add $50 to $100 for after-hours or weekend service on top of the repair. We tell you about the fee before any work starts, so there are no surprises when the technician arrives.
Is it cheaper to repair or replace an old garage door?
Repair usually costs less in the short term. But if your door is 15 to 20 years old and the repairs would cost more than half of a new door, replacing it is usually better value over time. Our technicians will give you an honest comparison before you decide.
Do you offer free estimates before starting work?
Yes. Estimates are free and come with no obligation, and you'll know the full cost before any work begins. Nothing gets added without your approval.
What factors affect the total cost of a garage door repair?
The problem itself, the parts it needs, the size and material of the door, and whether it is a regular appointment or an after-hours emergency. Fixing one spring costs far less than replacing an opener or a whole door.
How long does a typical garage door repair take?
Most common repairs, like springs or cables, take about an hour. Installing a full door or opener takes a few hours, depending on the job.
