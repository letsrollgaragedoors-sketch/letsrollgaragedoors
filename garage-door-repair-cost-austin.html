Spring Replacement: The Most Common Repair

Broken torsion springs are the single most common reason a garage door stops working. Spring replacement starts at $249 for a single spring and $399 for a two-spring system. We always recommend replacing both springs at the same time since they wear at the same rate. Two service calls always costs more than replacing both in one visit, and the second spring is usually close to failure by the time the first one breaks.

Opener Repair vs. New Installation

Opener issues range from minor fixes like sensor alignment or remote reprogramming at $75 to $125, to full motor replacement at $150 to $250. If your opener is older and repair costs are approaching the cost of a new unit, a new opener installation starting at $459 often makes more financial sense. Modern openers are quieter, smarter, and more reliable than units from 10 or more years ago.

Cable Replacement

Lift cables run from the bottom corners of the door up to the spring drums. They typically come in pairs and should be replaced together. Cable replacement runs $100 to $200 and includes re-tensioning the spring system after installation. Cable failures often follow spring failures, so if your cable snapped, we will inspect the springs as part of the service call.

Roller Replacement

Worn plastic rollers are one of the most overlooked repair items. Starting at $99 for a full set, roller replacement is one of the best value repairs on the list. The immediate difference in how quietly the door operates is usually noticeable the moment we finish. We install nylon rollers as the standard upgrade over the plastic rollers most doors came with from the factory.

Emergency Service Fees

Most companies, including Let's Roll, add a $50 to $100 after-hours or weekend service fee on top of the repair cost. We are upfront about this before we dispatch a technician. You will know the full expected cost before anyone arrives.

When to Repair vs. Replace

If your door is over 15 to 20 years old and repairs would cost more than 50 percent of the cost of a new door, replacement is usually the smarter long-term investment. Our technicians will always give you an honest comparison before you decide. We would rather lose a repair job and earn your trust than sell you a repair on a door that should be replaced.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover garage door repair?
It depends on the cause. Damage from a storm, fire, or vehicle impact is often covered, while normal wear and tear from age usually is not. Check with your insurance provider about your specific policy before assuming coverage.
How much does emergency garage door repair cost after hours?
Most companies, including us, add a $50 to $100 after-hours or weekend service fee on top of the repair cost. We are upfront about this fee before any work begins so there are no surprises when the technician arrives.
Is it cheaper to repair or replace an old garage door?
Repair is generally less expensive in the short term. However, if your door is over 15 to 20 years old and repairs would cost more than half the cost of a new door, replacement usually provides better long-term value. Our technicians will give you an honest comparison before you decide.
Do you offer free estimates before starting work?
Yes. We provide free, no-obligation estimates with transparent, upfront pricing so you know the full cost before any work begins. Nothing gets added without your approval.
What factors affect the total cost of a garage door repair?
Cost depends on the specific issue, the parts needed, the door size and material, and whether the job is a standard appointment or after-hours emergency service. A single spring fix costs far less than a full opener or door replacement.
How long does a typical garage door repair take?
Most common repairs such as spring or cable replacement take about an hour. Full door or opener installations typically take a few hours depending on the scope of the job.
