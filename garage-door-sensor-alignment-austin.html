How to Align Garage Door Safety Sensors

Door won't close and the opener light keeps blinking? Nine times out of ten it's the safety sensors. Here's how to clean, re-aim, and test them yourself in about ten minutes.

Every garage door opener made since 1993 has two photo-eye sensors mounted near the floor on either side of the opening. They project an invisible beam across the doorway, and if anything breaks that beam while the door is closing, the door stops and reverses. It's the system that keeps the door from closing on a child, a pet, or your car bumper. More quick checks are in our garage door troubleshooting guide.

The catch: when the sensors get bumped, dirty, or knocked out of alignment, the opener assumes something is in the way and refuses to close the door at all. The good news is that unlike springs and cables, sensor alignment involves no stored tension, it's one of the few garage door fixes we genuinely recommend homeowners try themselves first.

How to Align Your Sensors, Step by Step

Clear and Clean the Sensors

Look at the two small sensors mounted a few inches off the floor on each side of the door opening. Remove anything blocking the path between them (bins, cobwebs, leaves) and wipe both lenses with a soft, dry cloth. Dirt or a spider web is the single most common cause of a door that won't close.

Check the Indicator Lights

Each sensor has a small LED. On most brands, one side (the sending eye) glows steadily no matter what, while the other (the receiving eye) glows steadily only when the beam is connected. A blinking, dim, or dark receiving light means the beam is broken or the sensors are out of alignment.

Loosen and Re-Aim the Blinking Sensor

Loosen the wing nut or screw on the misaligned sensor's bracket just enough to pivot it by hand. Slowly angle it up, down, and side to side while watching its LED. When the light turns solid and stops flickering, hold it steady and retighten the nut, then confirm the light stays solid.

Check the Brackets and Wiring

Gently press each bracket, if it flexes or has been knocked crooked by a bumper or trash can, straighten it. Follow the thin wires from each sensor toward the opener and look for pinches, staples through insulation, or chewed sections. Loose or damaged wire causes intermittent failures that look like alignment problems.

Test the Door

Press the wall button to close the door and let it run untouched. Then test the safety reversal: with the door closing, wave an object through the beam, the door should stop and reverse immediately. If it does, you're done.

When to Call a Professional

If both sensor lights are solid and the door still won't close, if the wiring is damaged, or if the lights won't come on at all, the fault is likely in the opener itself rather than the alignment. Those repairs involve opener-specific force and limit adjustments or board-level diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my garage door sensors are misaligned?

The classic symptom: the door won't close from the remote or wall button and either reverses immediately or the opener light flashes several times. If the door only closes when you press and hold the wall button, the opener is telling you the sensors can't see each other.

What color should garage door sensor lights be?

It varies by brand. On LiftMaster and Chamberlain, the sending eye is amber/yellow and the receiving eye is green; Genie uses red and green. The rule that matters: both lights should be solid, not blinking, when the path is clear.

Why does my door still not close after aligning the sensors?

If both LEDs are solid and the door still won't close, the problem is usually elsewhere: travel limits, closing force settings, a worn wall-button wire, or the opener's logic board. Those adjustments are opener-specific, and this is a good point to call (512) 947-4451 for a diagnosis.

Can I bypass my garage door sensors?

Holding the wall button will force the door closed in a pinch, but permanently bypassing sensors defeats a federally required safety system that prevents the door from closing on people, pets, and vehicles. Fix the sensors instead, alignment or replacement is a quick, inexpensive repair.

If cleaning and re-aiming doesn't solve it, a failed sensor pair is one of the least expensive garage door repairs, and we can usually replace them same-day. Call (512) 947-4451 for an exact quote for your opener brand.
