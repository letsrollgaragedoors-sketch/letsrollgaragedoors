Your garage door is the largest moving object in your home and most doors cycle over a thousand times a year. Springs stretch, hardware loosens, rollers dry out, and the opener quietly compensates until something finally gives, usually at the worst possible time.

Let's Roll Garage Doors offers garage door tune-ups and preventive maintenance in Austin, TX, a full-system service that keeps your door quiet, balanced, and safe, and extends the life of your springs and opener. Think of it like an oil change. You wouldn't skip oil changes and expect your car to keep running forever. A garage door opens and closes about 1,500 times a year. That is a lot of cycles on parts under heavy tension, in a city with big temperature swings.

What We Actually Find on Austin Service Calls

Austin's climate is harder on garage doors than most homeowners realize. Triple-digit summers, UV, seasonal temperature swings and humid spring storms all leave the same kinds of wear on door after door.

Loose Hardware Is Almost Universal

Every garage door shakes a little with every open and close. After a year of that, nuts, bolts and brackets work themselves loose. On most doors we service, at least a handful of fasteners need tightening. Left alone, loose hardware leads to rattling, track misalignment and eventually cracked panels from the extra stress.

Plastic Rollers Are Usually Past Their Best

Most doors built before 2015 came with plastic rollers. In Austin's heat they wear out faster than they would in cooler places. The plastic cracks, the bearing inside seizes, and the roller drags instead of rolling. A dragging roller adds load to every other part, especially the opener motor. We swap plastic rollers for nylon on a lot of tune-up calls, and the door runs quieter right away.

Springs Are Often Out of Balance

Springs lose tension slowly over thousands of cycles. On a door that has never been adjusted, one spring can be much more worn than the other, so the door lifts unevenly. That puts lopsided strain on the cables, tracks and opener. Adjusting the tension during a tune-up brings back even travel and helps everything connected to the springs last longer.

Safety Sensors Drift Out of Alignment

The photo-eye sensors at the bottom of the tracks get bumped by almost anything: a bag, a broom, a car door swung too wide. Once they're out of line, the door opens fine but won't close, or it reverses halfway down for no reason. We check the sensors on every tune-up and realign them if needed.

Weatherstripping Is Often Cracked or Flat

The rubber seal at the bottom of the door and the weatherstripping along the sides and top break down faster in Austin than in most places. UV hardens the rubber until it cracks. A door that doesn't seal well lets in hot air, water and bugs. We flag the condition of your seals on every tune-up so you know when it's time to replace them.

Why Austin Doors Need a Tune-Up More Than Most

In a mild climate, a garage door can go years between tune-ups without obvious trouble. Austin is not mild. The door expands on a 105-degree afternoon and shrinks on a cold winter night, again and again. That loosens fasteners, wears out seals and speeds up rust on uncoated springs faster than average.

Austin tip: If your torsion springs aren't galvanized or powder-coated, think about upgrading to corrosion-resistant ones the next time you replace them. Standard springs rust noticeably faster in Austin's humidity. The upgrade costs a little more and adds years of life.

Skipping your yearly maintenance doesn't mean nothing happens. It means the wear keeps building up unseen until something fails, usually at the worst possible moment.

Signs Your Door Is Overdue

Squealing or Grinding

Dry rollers and bearings get loud before they fail.

Jerky or Shaky Travel

Uneven movement points to balance, track, or roller issues.

Heavy to Lift by Hand

A balanced door should lift easily with one hand, if not, the springs are losing tension.

Slower Than It Used to Be

An opener straining against a poorly maintained door slows down before it burns out.

Won't Stay Open Halfway

Lift it to waist height and let go. If it falls fast or shoots up, it fails the balance test and the spring tension is off.

Reverses on Its Own

Misaligned sensors or opener sensitivity settings usually cause a door that reverses for no reason.

Over 12 Months Since the Last Service

Even if everything seems fine, once a year is the right schedule for Austin's climate.

How to Test Your Door's Balance Yourself

You can check one of the most important signs of your door's health in about 30 seconds.

Disconnect the Opener

Pull the red cord hanging from the opener trolley.

Lift to Waist Height

Lift the door by hand to roughly halfway up.

Let Go and Step Back

Let go completely and step back from the door.

Read the Result

A balanced door stays put or drifts down a little. If it falls fast or shoots up, the spring tension is off and a pro needs to adjust it.

Don't try to adjust spring tension yourself. Torsion springs are under extreme tension and can seriously hurt you if you handle them without the right tools and training. Always leave spring adjustment to a professional.

Repair or Replace After a Tune-Up?

Sometimes a tune-up shows that a door has bigger problems than maintenance can fix. If the door is structurally damaged, has several parts failing, or is 15 to 20 years old and badly worn, we'll tell you honestly whether it still makes sense to keep maintaining it or whether a replacement door is the better long-term choice. We would rather give you an honest answer than sell you a tune-up on a door that needs to be replaced.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a garage door be tuned up?

Once a year for most Austin homes. If your door cycles more than 5-6 times a day, or it's your home's main entrance, twice a year is worth it. Central Texas heat is hard on lubricant and weather seals, so many customers schedule before summer.

How long does a garage door tune-up take?

About 45 minutes to an hour for a standard residential door. If we find a worn part during the inspection, we'll show it to you and quote the fix before doing anything beyond the tune-up.

What's included in a garage door tune-up?

Lubrication of springs, rollers, hinges, and bearings; spring balance testing; tightening of all hardware; track alignment check; safety sensor and auto-reverse testing; opener force and travel-limit check; and a weather seal inspection.

Is a tune-up really worth it?

Yes, springs on an unbalanced door wear out significantly faster, and a strained opener works harder every cycle. A yearly tune-up is a fraction of the cost of an emergency spring replacement or a new opener, and it's the best way to catch small problems before they strand your car in the garage.

Can I just lubricate the door myself?

You can and should apply a silicone- or lithium-based garage door lubricant to rollers and hinges between visits (avoid WD-40, it's a solvent, not a lubricant). What a tune-up adds is the part you can't safely do yourself: testing and adjusting spring balance, which involves components under high tension.

How much does a garage door tune-up cost in Austin?

The price depends on the size and condition of your door. Call us for a free upfront quote before we start any work. We're open about cost from the start, with no hidden fees.

Is a tune-up necessary if my garage door seems to be working fine?

Yes. A weakening spring, a fraying cable or a misaligned sensor won't be obvious during normal use. The door still works, but other parts are carrying extra strain. A tune-up catches these problems before they turn into an emergency repair.

What is the difference between a tune-up and a repair?

A tune-up is preventive maintenance on a door that still works. A repair fixes something that has already failed. Tune-ups cost much less than most repairs and help you avoid the repair in the first place.

Can a tune-up prevent a broken spring?

Not always, because springs wear out eventually no matter what. But a tune-up lets us spot a spring that is losing tension and getting close to failure, so you can replace it on your own schedule and not in an emergency. It also keeps the spring system balanced, which cuts the stress that speeds up wear.

Do you lubricate the tracks during a tune-up?

No, and a lot of homeowners get this wrong. Tracks should stay clean and dry so the rollers grip properly. We lubricate the springs, hinges, rollers and the opener chain or drive screw, but not the tracks.
