Why Won't My Garage Door Open?

Broken Springs

When a spring breaks, the door can become extremely heavy or refuse to open entirely. Spring work is dangerous and should always be handled by a trained professional.

Broken or Frayed Cables

A broken or loose cable can make the door crooked, stuck, or unsafe to operate. Avoid repeatedly using the opener until the system is inspected.

Door Off Track

Rollers can come out of the track due to worn rollers, bent tracks, cable problems, or impact. Never force an off-track door open or closed. Learn about off-track repair.

Opener Problems

If the door appears intact but won't respond, the issue may involve the motor, remote, drive components, electrical supply, or other opener hardware.

Safety Sensor Issues

Misaligned or blocked photo-eye sensors are one of the most common reasons a working door refuses to close or open properly.

Damaged Panels & Hardware

Bent tracks, worn hinges, and damaged panels or brackets can bind the door and prevent smooth operation.

Is It the Door or the Opener? A 30-Second Test

This one test saves a lot of guessing. It tells you whether the problem is the door itself or the opener that runs it. It only works if the door is fully closed, so if your door is stuck open, skip it and call us.

Close the Door and Unplug the Opener

The door must be all the way down. Unplug the opener from the ceiling outlet, then pull the red release cord. The exact steps are in our guide on how to open a garage door manually.

Lift the Door by Hand

Use both hands on the bottom edge and lift slowly to about waist height. Do not touch the springs, cables or brackets.

Feel How It Moves

A healthy door feels light and stays up when you let go. Lower it back down gently when you are done.

What You Just Learned

The door feels very heavy, or it drops

That points to a broken spring or a cable problem. Stop, do not force it, and call us. See the broken spring warning signs.

The door lifts easily and stays up

The door is fine and the opener or its controls are the problem. Check the remote battery and the sensors first. If it still will not run, call us about opener repair.

One side is higher, or it jams in the track

That is usually a cable or an off-track roller. Do not keep forcing it. We cover this in our off-track repair.

Plug the opener back in and re-engage the release when you are finished. More things to try before you call are in our garage door troubleshooting guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why won't my garage door open?

Common causes include a broken spring, broken cable, off-track rollers, damaged tracks, opener problems, safety sensor issues, or other hardware failures.

Can I manually open a garage door that won't open?

A damaged garage door can be extremely heavy or unstable. If the door is crooked, unusually heavy, or has a broken spring or cable, avoid forcing it open and have it inspected.

Is it safe to keep using a garage door that won't open correctly?

It is best to stop using a door when there is a visible mechanical problem, unusual noise, a broken cable or spring, or an off-track condition.

How quickly can a garage door be repaired in Austin?

Let's Roll Garage Doors offers same-day service when availability allows. Call (512) 947-4451 to check service availability.

Should I repair or replace my garage door?

It depends on the door's age, condition, repair cost, and whether problems are recurring. A professional inspection can help you compare the options.

How much does garage door repair cost in Austin?

Cost depends on the failed component and the parts and labor required. Springs, cables, rollers, openers, tracks, and panels can all have different repair costs. We quote the price upfront before starting work.

How do I know if it is the door or the opener?

With the door fully closed, unplug the opener, pull the red release cord and lift the door by hand. If it feels light and stays up, the door is fine and the opener is the problem. If it feels very heavy or drops, the problem is a spring or cable, and you should stop and call a pro.
