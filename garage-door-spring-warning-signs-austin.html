7 Warning Signs Your Garage Door Spring Is About to Break

A broken torsion spring is one of the most common garage door failures, and one of the most dangerous. When it snaps, your door can stop working in an instant, leaving your car stuck inside or your home unsecured. The good news is that springs usually give you some warning first. When you're ready to get it fixed, take a look at our garage door spring repair in Austin page.

Never try to repair or replace a garage door spring yourself. Torsion springs are under extreme tension and can seriously hurt or even kill you if you handle them without the right tools and training. Always call a certified technician.

Why Springs Fail and Why Austin Makes It Worse

A standard torsion spring is rated for about 10,000 cycles. If your household opens the door 4 times a day, that works out to roughly 7 years. Austin's weather can shorten that. Summer highs above 100 degrees, then cold snaps that sometimes dip below freezing, keep stressing the metal and wear springs out faster than the rating suggests. Spring-season humidity also speeds up rust on uncoated springs. We replace springs in Austin more often than the national average would predict, and many of the failures come when the weather swings hard, like a sudden cold snap in January or February.

The 7 Warning Signs

Sign 1

The Door Feels Unusually Heavy

Springs do the work of balancing the door's weight. When one is weakening or partly broken, the door feels much heavier than it should when you lift it by hand. A balanced door lifts easily with one hand. If it takes real effort, that is a red flag. Disconnect the opener and try lifting the door yourself. It is the quickest way to catch a failing spring before it goes.

Sign 2

You Hear a Loud Bang

When a torsion spring snaps, it sounds like a gunshot inside the garage. If you hear that and the door won't open afterward, the spring has almost certainly broken. Don't try to run the door with the opener. Look above the door for a gap in the spring coil to confirm it.

Sign 3

There Is a Visible Gap in the Spring

Look at the torsion spring mounted sideways above your door. A broken one has a gap in the coil, usually 2 inches or more, where the metal has split apart. If you see that, stop using the door right away and call for service. It is about the clearest sign you'll ever get that a spring has failed.

Sign 4

The Door Opens Crooked

If one spring fails in a two-spring system, the door goes up unevenly, with one side higher than the other. That lopsided load puts a lot of strain on the cables, tracks and opener, and it can cause more damage fast. Don't keep running a crooked door. The longer you do, the more it breaks.

Sign 5

The Opener Strains or Fails

Your opener is built to work alongside a healthy spring system. If it sounds like it's struggling, reverses right after it starts lifting, or hits its safety stop, a failing spring is often the cause. The opener usually isn't the problem. It is sensing that the load is too heavy and shutting off to protect itself.

Sign 6

Cables Are Loose or Hanging

A broken spring lets the lift cables go slack or dangle at the sides of the door. Loose cables are both a symptom and a hazard. They can snap or tangle when the door moves, which causes more damage and could even make the door drop. If you see loose cables, stop using the door.

Sign 7

The Spring Looks Stretched, Spaced Out or Rusty

Over time springs lose tension and the coils spread apart. If the gaps between coils look wider than they used to, the spring is wearing out. Rust on the coils is another warning sign. In Austin's humidity, uncoated springs corrode faster than they would in a drier place, and corrosion weakens the metal and speeds up failure.

How Long Do Garage Door Springs Last?

Standard torsion springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles, which is roughly 7 years for an average household. High-cycle springs, rated for 25,000 to 50,000 cycles, are available and last a lot longer. If your springs are getting close to 7 years old, ask about high-cycle replacements when it's time. The price difference is small compared to the extra years you get.

Austin note: If you have two springs and one breaks, replace both. They went in at the same time and have the same number of cycles on them, so the second one is usually close to failing when the first one goes. Replacing both together (starting at $499) costs far less than two separate service calls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I replace a garage door spring myself?
No. Torsion springs are under extreme tension and can cause serious injury or death if you handle them without the right tools and training. This is one repair to always leave to a certified technician.
How much does it cost to replace a broken garage door spring in Austin?
A single torsion spring replacement starts at $299, and a two-spring system starts at $499. We always recommend replacing both springs together, since they wear at the same rate.
How long do garage door springs typically last?
Standard torsion springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles, which is roughly 7 years for an average household. High-cycle springs rated for 25,000 to 50,000 cycles are available and last considerably longer.
Is it safe to use my garage door if a spring is broken?
No. With a broken spring the door loses its counterbalance. That puts extra strain on the opener and cables, and the door could actually drop. Stay off the door and call for service.
What is the difference between torsion springs and extension springs?
Torsion springs mount sideways above the door and twist to store energy. Extension springs run along the upper tracks and stretch. Torsion springs are more common on newer doors, and they are generally considered safer and longer-lasting.
How quickly should I get a spring replaced once I notice warning signs?
As soon as you can. Once a spring starts showing wear, it can fail with very little notice. That is exactly why we offer 24/7 emergency service in Austin.
