7 Warning Signs Your Garage Door Spring Is About to Break

Dudu E., Owner, Let's Roll Garage Doors June 2, 2026 5 min read Austin, TX

A broken torsion spring is one of the most common and most dangerous garage door failures. When a spring snaps, your door can become completely inoperable in seconds, leaving your vehicle trapped or your home unsecured. The good news is that springs usually give you warning signs before they fail entirely.

Never attempt to repair or replace garage door springs yourself. Torsion springs are under extreme tension and can cause serious injury or death if handled without the right tools and training. Always call a certified technician.

Why Springs Fail and Why Austin Makes It Worse

Standard torsion springs are rated for approximately 10,000 cycles. For a household that opens the garage door 4 times a day, that works out to roughly 7 years. But Austin's climate accelerates that timeline. The thermal cycling between summer highs above 100 degrees and winter lows that occasionally dip below freezing stresses metal and causes springs to fatigue faster than the cycle rating suggests. Humidity during spring storm season accelerates corrosion on uncoated springs. We replace springs in Austin more frequently per year than the national average, and most failures cluster in January and February right after the first hard freeze of the season.

The 7 Warning Signs

Sign 1

The Door Feels Unusually Heavy

Springs counterbalance the weight of your garage door. If a spring is weakening or partially broken, the door will feel much heavier than normal when you lift it manually. A properly balanced door should lift easily with one hand. If it takes real effort, that is a red flag. Disconnect the opener and try lifting by hand this is the single fastest way to detect a failing spring before it breaks completely.

Sign 2

You Hear a Loud Bang

When a torsion spring snaps, it makes a sound like a gunshot going off inside the garage. If you hear this and your door will not open afterward, your spring has almost certainly broken. Do not try to operate the door with the opener. Look above the door for a visible gap in the spring coil that confirms it.

Sign 3

There Is a Visible Gap in the Spring

Look at the torsion spring mounted horizontally above your door. A broken spring will have a visible gap in the coil typically 2 inches or more where the metal has separated. If you see this, stop using the door immediately and call for service. This is one of the clearest signs you will ever get that a spring has failed.

Sign 4

The Door Opens Crooked

If one spring fails in a two-spring system, the door will rise unevenly one side will be higher than the other. This asymmetric load puts enormous strain on the cables, tracks, and opener and can cause secondary damage quickly. Do not keep operating an uneven door. The longer it runs in this condition, the more collateral damage accumulates.

Sign 5

The Opener Strains or Fails

Your opener is designed to work with a properly functioning spring system. If the opener sounds like it is struggling, reverses immediately after starting to lift, or triggers its safety stop, a failing spring is often the cause. The opener is not the problem it is detecting that the load is too heavy and stopping to protect itself.

Sign 6

Cables Are Loose or Hanging

Broken springs cause the lift cables to go slack or hang freely on the sides of the door. Loose cables are both a symptom and a danger they can snap or tangle if the door is operated, causing additional damage and potentially causing the door to drop. If you see loose cables, stop using the door.

Sign 7

The Spring Looks Stretched, Spaced, or Corroded

Over time, springs lose tension and the coils spread apart. If the gaps between coils look visibly wider than they used to, the spring is losing tension and approaching end of life. Rust or surface corrosion on the spring coils is also a warning sign in Austin's humidity, uncoated springs corrode faster than in drier climates, and corrosion weakens the metal and accelerates failure.

How Long Do Garage Door Springs Last?

Standard torsion springs are rated for approximately 10,000 cycles roughly 7 years for an average household. High-cycle springs rated for 25,000 to 50,000 cycles are available and significantly extend that timeline. If your springs are approaching 7 years old, it is worth asking about high-cycle replacements when the time comes the cost difference is modest compared to the extra years of service life.

Austin note: If you have a two-spring system and one breaks, replace both. They were installed at the same time and have the same cycle count. The second spring is usually close to failure by the time the first one goes. Replacing both at once (starting at $399) costs far less than two separate service calls.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I replace a garage door spring myself?
No. Torsion springs are under extreme tension and can cause severe injury or death if handled without the right tools and training. This is one repair that should always be left to a certified technician.
How much does it cost to replace a broken garage door spring in Austin?
Single torsion spring replacement starts at $249. A two-spring system starts at $399. We always recommend replacing both springs at the same time since they wear at the same rate.
How long do garage door springs typically last?
Standard torsion springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles, roughly 7 years for an average household. High-cycle springs rated for 25,000 to 50,000 cycles are available and last considerably longer.
Is it safe to use my garage door if a spring is broken?
No. A broken spring means the door has lost its counterbalance, putting excess strain on the opener and cables and creating a real risk of the door dropping. Avoid using the door and call for service.
What is the difference between torsion springs and extension springs?
Torsion springs mount horizontally above the door and twist to store energy. Extension springs run along the upper horizontal tracks and stretch. Torsion springs are more common on newer doors and generally considered safer and longer-lasting.
How quickly should I get a spring replaced once I notice warning signs?
As soon as possible. Once a spring shows signs of wear, it can fail with little further warning. We offer 24/7 emergency service in Austin for exactly this reason.
