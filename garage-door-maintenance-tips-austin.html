5 Easy Garage Door Maintenance Tips That Add Years to Its Life

Let's Roll Garage Doors

Your garage door is probably the biggest moving thing in your house, and it opens and closes 1,500 times or more every year. In Austin's heat, its parts wear out faster than they would almost anywhere else. These five jobs take less than 30 minutes in total, and they can save you hundreds of dollars in repairs.

Why Maintenance Matters More in Austin

Most garage door advice is written for an average climate, and Austin isn't one. Triple-digit summers, UV, big temperature swings and humid spring storms all wear down rubber seals, metal springs, plastic rollers and hardware faster. A door that could go 3 years without attention somewhere mild needs a look every 6 to 12 months here.

The 5 Tasks

Tip 1

Lubricate the Moving Parts Every 6 Months

Use white lithium grease or a silicone-based lubricant on the springs, hinges, rollers and the opener chain or drive screw. Coat the whole length of each spring, then swing each hinge back and forth a few times to work the lubricant in. Skip the WD-40. It is a solvent, not a lubricant, and over time it strips the protective coating off your springs.

What not to lubricate: the tracks. They should stay clean and dry so the rollers can grip. Grease on the tracks makes rollers slip and the door travel unevenly.

Tip 2

Test the Balance Monthly

Pull the red cord to disconnect the opener, then lift the door by hand to about waist height and let go. A well-balanced door stays put or drifts down a tiny bit. If it drops fast or shoots up, the springs have lost their tension and a pro needs to adjust them.

Please don't try to adjust the springs yourself. They hold enough energy to seriously hurt you, and the job takes proper winding bars and experience. If the door fails this test, give us a call.

Tip 3

Inspect and Replace Weather Stripping

Look at the rubber seal along the bottom of the door and the weatherstripping on the sides and top. In Austin, plan to replace the bottom seal every 2 to 3 years, because UV hardens and cracks rubber much faster here than in cooler places. A cracked seal lets in hot air, water, bugs and other pests. A new bottom seal costs under $30 at a hardware store and slides into the existing channel in a few minutes.

Tip 4

Tighten All the Hardware

Thousands of open and close cycles shake nuts, bolts and brackets loose over time. Grab a socket wrench and tighten everything you can reach, especially the lag bolts that hold the track brackets to the wall and the bolts on the hinges between panels. Loose track brackets are one of the most common reasons we get called out for an off-track door in Austin.

Tip 5

Test Your Safety Sensors

Lay a 2x4 flat on the floor in the path of the door and try to close it. The door should reverse the moment it touches the board. If it doesn't, the auto-reverse isn't working. That is a safety issue, not an optional feature, so get it looked at right away. While you're down there, check the photo-eye sensors near the bottom of each track. Both little lights should be solid. A blinking light means the sensors are out of line.

Pro tip: Have a professional tune-up done once a year. Our technicians spot things you'd never catch on your own, like a fraying cable, a weakening spring or a seized roller bearing. It costs far less than an emergency repair call.

How Often to Do Each Task

  • Monthly: Balance test, plus a quick look at the springs and cables
  • Every 6 months: Lubricate all moving parts, tighten hardware, test the safety sensors
  • Every 2 to 3 years in Austin: Replace the bottom seal and weatherstripping
  • Once a year: Professional tune-up

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I lubricate my garage door?
Every 6 months is a good rule for most Austin homes. If the door gets heavy daily use, or it starts making new noises, going more often won't hurt.
What type of lubricant should I use?
White lithium grease or a silicone-based lubricant on the springs, hinges, rollers and the opener chain or drive screw. Avoid WD-40, because it is a solvent, not a lubricant, and it breaks down your springs over time. Never put lubricant on the tracks.
How do I know if my garage door is unbalanced?
Disconnect the opener and lift the door by hand to about halfway. A balanced door stays where you leave it. If it falls or shoots up, the spring tension needs a professional adjustment.
Can I test my garage door safety sensors myself?
Yes. Lay a 2x4 flat on the floor in the door's path and try to close it. The door should reverse as soon as it touches the board. If it doesn't, call for service, because it is a safety issue.
How often should I schedule a professional tune-up?
Once a year is the usual advice. A pro catches wear and loose hardware that are easy to miss on a DIY check, and it costs far less than an emergency repair.
