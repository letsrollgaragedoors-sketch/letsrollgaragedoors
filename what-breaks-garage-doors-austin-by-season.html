What Breaks on Austin Garage Doors, Season by Season

Dudu Elmakies, Owner, Let's Roll Garage Doors October 22, 2026 5 min read Austin, TX

The short version: In Austin, springs and rollers tend to give out in the summer heat and during sudden weather swings. Openers get hurt by spring storms, and sensors act up when the sun sits low in spring and fall. This comes from what our technicians see on service calls around town, not from a formal study. Here is what breaks each season, why, and a quick check you can do yourself.

Which Garage Door Parts Fail in Which Season in Austin?

Garage doors don't break at random. Heat, humidity, storms, low sun and sudden temperature swings each hit different parts. Here is how that lines up with the calls we get most. It is our field experience across Austin and nearby cities, and the "why" column is just basic physics.

Why Do Garage Door Springs Break in Summer and During Drastic Weather Changes?

The spring does the heavy lifting. It stores the energy that raises your door, and it wears out by use, not by season. Most standard springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles (one open plus one close), and longer-life springs for 20,000 or more. Weather doesn't cause that wear, but it often decides the day a worn spring finally snaps.

  • Summer: A garage can get a lot hotter than the air outside, especially with a west-facing door or an attic overhead. Heat dries out lubricant and adds friction, so a tired spring has to work harder until it quits.
  • Drastic weather changes: Austin can go from warm to near freezing in a day or two. Metal shrinks as it cools, and the spring and its grease stiffen up. A sudden swing, like the hard freeze during Winter Storm Uri in February 2021, tends to finish off springs that were already close to done.
Five-minute check: With the door closed, look at the spring above it. Rust, a gap in the coils or a stretched look are all bad signs. Then pull the red release cord and lift the door by hand. If it feels heavy or won't stay halfway up, the spring is losing strength. Please don't touch the spring itself. Call us instead.

Why Do Garage Door Openers Fail During Storm Season?

Spring storms in Central Texas bring lightning, brownouts and power surges. Your opener plugs into a ceiling outlet, and the circuit board inside doesn't handle surges well. When it goes, you usually see one of three things: the opener won't respond, it forgets its settings, or the remotes stop working. Humidity can also corrode the terminals and the wall button wiring.

  • Plug the opener into a surge protector, or choose a model with battery backup.
  • After any outage, run the door all the way up and down once, then lay a board under it to check that it reverses.
  • If the opener hums but the door doesn't move, the spring may be the real problem. See our pages on opener repair and the complete opener guide.

Why Do Garage Door Sensors Stop Working at Certain Times of Day?

The safety sensors near the floor send an invisible beam across the opening. In spring and fall the sun sits low enough to shine right into a lens, and the opener reads that as something blocking the door. So the door won't close, or it reverses right away, and it often happens at the same time every day.

  • Wipe both lenses and make sure both little lights are solid.
  • Shade the sunny sensor with a short cardboard tube or hood.
  • If one light is blinking, the sensors are out of line. Our guide on aligning garage door sensors walks through it.

What Happens to Rollers, Seals and Hardware Over an Austin Year?

These are the slow problems. Nobody notices them until something stops working. Plastic rollers crack from UV and heat. Rubber bottom seals go hard and stop touching the floor. Bolts work loose after months of daily expanding and shrinking, and a loose track bracket can eventually put the door off track. We cover seals and how often to replace them in our guide to Austin heat and humidity.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do garage door springs break most often in Austin?
From what we see, there are two busy stretches. One is the middle of summer, when a spring that has been working for years finally gives up in the heat. The other is any time the weather swings hard, like a sudden cold snap after a warm week. Age matters more than the calendar, though. A spring that is close to the end of its life can go in any month.
Why does my garage door opener act up during Texas storms?
Lightning and power surges can fry an opener's circuit board or wipe its settings. A surge protector helps, and so does a battery backup. After any outage, run the door up and down once and make sure it still reverses when it hits something.
Why do my garage door sensors stop working in the afternoon?
Low sun can shine straight into a sensor lens and fool it into thinking something is in the way. The door then won't close, or it closes a little and goes right back up. It happens most in spring and fall when the sun sits low. A small cardboard shade over the sensor often fixes it. Dirty lenses and bumped alignment are the other usual causes.
How many cycles is a garage door spring rated for?
Most standard torsion springs are rated for about 10,000 cycles, and one cycle is one open and one close. Longer-life springs are often rated for 20,000 or more. If your family uses the door several times a day, a standard spring can run out in roughly 7 to 10 years.
What is the best time of year for a garage door tune-up in Austin?
Early spring and early fall. Spring is a good time to catch worn seals and tired springs before summer, and fall lets you deal with summer damage before the weather turns.
